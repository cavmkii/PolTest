            Scoring

            Answers count +1, +½, 0, −½, −1. Each statement carries a weight on one or more axes; your axis score is the weighted sum divided by the largest possible sum, scaled to −100…+100. Skipped statements drop out of both. Each axis has statements in both directions so that agreeing with everything does not push you to one pole.

            Matches use root-mean-square distance over the axes both profiles define, converted to a percentage where 0 distance is 100% and the maximum possible distance (200) is 0%. Figures need a score on at least 5 axes to enter the overall ranking. Per-axis matches use any figure scored on that axis, with ties broken by overall distance.

            How the figures were scored

            The roster is deliberately limited to divisive figures: dictators, militant and terrorist leaders, and notorious public figures. Positions are inferred from what each person did in power and said in their own writings or speeches, placed on the present-day spectrum you answer on, not relative to their era.

            Because nobody mainstream is on the list, a moderate will still get a "closest" match, usually a far worse person than they are. The percentage tells you how close that really is.

            Confidence reflects how much political record exists. Bin Laden, Lenin and Thatcher wrote or governed at length. Sean Combs and Jeffrey Epstein left almost no stated politics: their profiles cover the few axes with evidence (donations, campaigns, public statements) and are blank elsewhere. Epstein falls below the 5-axis threshold and is excluded from overall rankings. Matching someone on an axis is a fact about one position, not about character or conduct.

            Limits

            Ten axes cannot hold a whole politics. A single number for "Means" flattens the difference between Gandhi's nonviolent law-breaking and a bombing campaign; the statements try to separate those but the scale cannot. Ideology profiles are idealized centers, and real movements spread wide around them. Statement wording carries bias that no balancing fully removes. Treat the output as a map for argument, not a measurement.
